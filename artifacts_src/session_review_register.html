Two different questions

Verdicts applied means the good / MUA / noise calls were written into the group column of cluster_info.tsv — never into cluster_group.tsv, and never by hand.

Bpod synced means scripts/sync_session.py wrote a synced/ directory aligning the ephys to the behaviour. A session can have one without the other, and treating them as one question is how “is it synced?” gets answered wrongly.

What the states mean

saved, complete
Every unit judged, and all three collections written to Z.
saved, partial
Reviewing is still in progress, but everything decided so far is on Z.
drifted
The review page holds more decisions than the file on Z. Those extra ones exist only in a browser database, which is one cleared cache away from gone. Nothing is in this state today.
not reviewed
Sorted, but no verdicts anywhere — either no review page has been built or the page is open and empty.
superseded
A standalone sort of one part of a day that was later joined and sorted once. It is kept as a second opinion, is not for review, and its unit ids do not correspond to the joint sort's.

For the Bpod sync: split spike_times at sample 124,350,000 and sync each part against its own Bpod file. A spike at concat sample s is part 1 at sample s when s is below the seam, and part 2 at s − 124,350,000 otherwise. The same offset applies to the DIO trial markers, read from each .rec separately and then shifted.

The seam is a break in time, not in the array. Anything that bins over time, computes a rate, or looks for drift must treat sample 124,350,000 as a 37 min 56 s gap and never interpolate across it. One Kilosort batch straddles it, and its spikes are worth checking for a rate artefact.

Keeping this current

Verdicts live in each review page's own database on claude.ai. Nothing on disk knows about them until they are dumped, so this register compares the two and says drifted when the page is ahead of Z.

Say the word after a reviewing session and the three collections get dumped to <session>.kilosort/review/verdicts/ on Z, hashed on both sides, and this table is regenerated. The markdown copy lives at post_ks_correction/SESSION_STATUS.md so git carries its history.

Everything in the provenance blocks is read out of each session's own concat_*.json, so a note here cannot disagree with what was actually sorted.
